# Proposed tolerance and its limits

The comparator tests consistency with a pinned analytic construction. It cannot certify the historical writer or approve the converter's real profile. Its eight-ULP threshold is fixed before any real buffer is inspected. ULP here means the integer distance between finite IEEE binary32 representations with the same sign. Every reference is normal, nonzero and far from an exponent overflow boundary; zero, sign changes and nonfinite encodings cannot pass.

For `n_`, let binary32 unit roundoff be u = 2^-24. Under round-to-nearest binary32 arithmetic, the integers -125 through -1 and 16000 are exactly representable. Rounding the division, rounding the scalar 2*pi and rounding the multiplication yield relative error at most `(1+u)^3-1` against the ideal expression. Allowing the reference's final rounding gives a bound of about four representable steps; eight is a conservative margin for this stated arithmetic model. The synthetic comparison covers division-first, scalar-rounded and scalar-unrounded order variants, plus a double-intermediate variant. It does not run historical Torch kernels.

The Hamming reference is `0.54 - 0.46*cos(2*pi*i/250)`, i = 0..124, rounded to binary32. This is the conventional NumPy definition. Its smallest reference is about 0.08, with binary32 spacing 2^-27. A sufficient numerical assumption for this check is that each implementation's pre-cast evaluation differs from the mathematical formula by at most 2^-28 absolute, followed by ordinary nearest binary32 rounding. Under that assumption the two evaluations differ by at most about two representable steps, within eight. This is an explicit sufficient assumption, **not a bound established for every historical cosine kernel**. The independent stdlib examples compare the direct formula and the algebraically equivalent centered cosine formula, without importing NumPy. [NumPy Hamming definition](https://numpy.org/doc/2.2/reference/generated/numpy.hamming.html)

Approximate division, nonstandard rounding, fast-math cosine, changed default dtype followed by other operations, a different filterbank implementation, or a modified buffer may violate the proposed bound. A source review of Python expressions alone cannot exclude these. Any future refusal must remain a refusal; increasing tolerance after seeing the artifact would require a new written basis and independent review.

The source registers both values as buffers and uses them in filter construction; only low_hz_ and band_hz_ are initialized as learned Parameters there. Buffer registration does not prove immutability. A malicious or edited checkpoint could deliberately satisfy these two arrays while changing other storage bytes. Consistency therefore supplies a chosen interpretation basis for these two arrays only. Applying its orientation to the other 21 selected storages would additionally rely on the uniform raw-storage writer contract and the fixed artifact identity; it is an explicit protocol assumption, not a consequence of these 250 comparisons.

The proposed decision requires every element of both buffers and exactly one common orientation. It rejects an ambiguous or unmatched result and does not compare average errors. A big-endian result cannot pass the frozen converter, whose source profile accepts little-endian only. Observed archive/version, full file/ZIP/CRC bindings, appropriate model notices, migration approval and runtime qualification remain separate work.
