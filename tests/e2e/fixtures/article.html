Field notes from a made-up workshop

By Fixture Writer

This page exists only for the Uoink smoke suite. Every name, number and place in it is invented, and nothing here describes a real event, company or person.

The workshop opened with a simple question: how do small teams keep track of what they learn when the learning happens in videos, podcasts, posts and pages all at once? Most people in the room kept a folder of links and admitted they never opened it again.

What we tried

We spent the first hour testing what we called the saffron lantern protocol. Each person saved three things they had read that week, wrote one sentence about why, and filed it on a shelf with a plain name. Nobody was allowed to invent a new shelf until three items needed it.

The second hour was about finding things again. We searched for a phrase someone remembered from a talk, and timed how long it took to land on the exact moment. Plain words beat clever tags every time.

What we learned

  • Short shelf names get used. Long ones get ignored.
  • A saved item without its text is almost useless a month later.
  • People trust search more when it shows the quote, not just the title.

We closed with a promise to review the shelves once a week and to delete anything nobody could explain. The notes above are the whole record of the day, kept short on purpose.
